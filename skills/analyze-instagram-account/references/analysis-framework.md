# Analysis framework

## Content pillars

1. Skim all captions once before naming anything.
2. Name 4–7 pillars by *what the post is about and why someone would watch it* (topic + intent), e.g. "Tool tutorials (teach)", "Behind the scenes (relate)", "Hot takes (provoke)", "Offers/launches (sell)".
3. Pillars must be mutually exclusive; if a post fits two, pick its primary purpose.
4. Keep "Other" under 10%; if larger, split out a new pillar.
5. Tag every post. Then compute per pillar: count, share %, median engagement, median views (Reels), best format.
6. Flag over-performers: pillars whose median engagement is ≥1.3× the account median, and under-performers ≤0.7×.

Useful intent lenses: Educate · Entertain · Inspire · Relate (personal/BTS) · Prove (results, testimonials) · Sell · Community (UGC, Q&A, collabs).

## Formats & hooks

- Format mix and median engagement per format (from metrics).
- Carousels: median slide count; note if longer carousels do better.
- Reels: median views; views-to-engagement ratio signals watch-vs-interact content.
- Hooks: the first caption line. Use `hook_types` counts, then read the top 10 posts' hooks and describe the patterns in your own words (question, number list, bold claim, "how I…", pain point, curiosity gap).
- Captions: length bucket that performs best, hashtag count, CTA usage and whether CTA posts outperform.

## Engagement & cadence

- Posts/week, median gap, longest gap (consistency).
- Best weekday/hour by post count and, where n ≥ 5 per bucket, by median engagement (compute in code).
- Trend: recent-half vs older-half median engagement → growing, flat, declining.
- Top 10 vs bottom 5 posts: what the winners share (pillar, format, hook type, length) that the losers lack.

## Report structure (single account)

1. **Summary** — what they make, for whom, and what drives their best results (3–4 sentences).
2. **Account snapshot** — followers, posts analysed, date range, posts/week, median engagement / ER.
3. **Content pillars** — table: pillar · definition · share · median engagement · best format; then 2–3 sentences of interpretation.
4. **Formats** — table + interpretation.
5. **Hooks & captions** — patterns, 3–5 example top hooks (short excerpts, linked), CTA and hashtag habits.
6. **Cadence & timing** — consistency, best days/times, trend.
7. **What wins vs what flops** — top vs bottom contrast.
8. **What to borrow** — 5–7 actionable ideas tied to findings.
9. **Method & caveats** — data source, sample size, limits.

## Comparison report structure (multiple accounts)

1. **Summary** — who leads on what, and the one gap the user can exploit.
2. **Snapshot table** — one row per account: followers, posts analysed, posts/week, median engagement, median ER, top format, top pillar.
3. **Pillar map** — normalise pillar names across accounts into shared categories; table of share % per account. Highlight white space (topics nobody covers well).
4. **Format & hook comparison** — format mix per account, dominant hook styles.
5. **Cadence comparison** — frequency and consistency.
6. **Standout tactics per account** — 2–3 bullets each.
7. **Opportunities for you** — 5–7 ideas, each naming the evidence.
8. **Method & caveats.**

Compare engagement **rates**, not raw counts, when follower sizes differ by more than 2×.
